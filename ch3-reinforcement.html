How to Use This Reinforcement Set

These exercises are formative — for building mastery after the Chapter 3 lecture, not for grading. All items are HOTS-level (applying, analyzing, evaluating, creating), most in Case Recitation style, and use fresh scenarios not found in the Mastery Test. Answer each item — for essays, write the full IRAC response — before opening the dropdown to compare with the model answer.

I.Reinforcement Multiple Choice — 20 HOTS Items

Question 1 · Applying

A researcher's compliance section cites "the DRRM law, as explained in my professor's slides." Under this chapter's sourcing discipline, the citation should instead be:

  1. Left as is — slides are authoritative for students
  2. Replaced with the primary text: the specific section of RA 10121 (Official Gazette or an authoritative repository), since compliance claims must be checkable against the rule as enacted
  3. Replaced with a link to a news explainer
  4. Deleted — compliance sections need no sources
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. Slides and summaries are teaching aids, not rules; a reviewer verifying a compliance claim must be able to open the cited provision and match it against your workplan. This is the same primary-source discipline as the Case Analysis template's "Based on Section __ of RA ____…" A, C, D each substitute convenience for verifiability.

Question 2 · Analyzing

Congress amends a statute; the old IRR now conflicts with the amended law in one procedure. Until a new IRR is issued, an agency should:

  1. Follow the old IRR — procedures never expire
  2. Follow the amended statute; the conflicting IRR provision is inoperative to the extent of the conflict, while unaffected IRR provisions continue to apply
  3. Suspend all operations indefinitely
  4. Choose whichever rule benefits the agency
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. The hierarchy resolves transitions the same way it resolves conflicts: the statute controls, the subordinate instrument yields exactly where it conflicts, and survives where it does not. A inverts the hierarchy; C overreacts; D describes the absence of a legal system. Researchers meet this pattern whenever funding guidelines lag behind amended laws.

Question 3 · Applying

A barangay wants to fund a community rain-gauge and flood-marker network — installation, training, and a student data-logging partnership — from its share of DRRM resources. Under RA 10121's fund logic, this proposal is:

  1. Ineligible — only relief goods qualify
  2. Eligible in principle: monitoring and early-warning work is prevention/mitigation and preparedness activity, the category the 70% (non-QRF) portion exists to support, programmed through the local DRRM plan
  3. Eligible only during typhoon season
  4. Ineligible because students are involved
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. The 70% share funds pre-disaster risk reduction — precisely where monitoring networks, hazard mapping, and capacity-building sit — and access runs through the LGU's own DRRM planning process (partnership, not grant application). A repeats the response-only paradigm the Act replaced; C and D invent restrictions with no statutory basis.

Question 4 · Evaluating

A councilor argues: "Our town has never flooded; our DRRM fund should buy an ambulance instead, since health emergencies are our real risk." Evaluate under the DRRM paradigm.

  1. Wholly invalid — DRRM funds may never touch health
  2. The premise misuses risk logic: risk = hazard × exposure × vulnerability requires assessment, not anecdote ("never flooded" ≠ no hazard); DRRM funds must serve DRRM purposes under the local plan, though risk-assessed preparedness (which can include emergency-response capability) may legitimately be programmed
  3. Wholly valid — LGUs know their risks best
  4. Valid if the ambulance is painted with DRRM markings
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. The strongest answer corrects the reasoning, not just the conclusion: the paradigm demands evidence-based risk assessment (science's statutory role), and fund use follows the DRRM plan built on that assessment. A is too absolute (preparedness can encompass response capability); C mistakes intuition for assessment; D is compliance theater.

Question 5 · Analyzing

Why does it matter to a hazard-science proposer that the NDRRMC's Prevention and Mitigation pillar is vice-chaired by DOST specifically, rather than by a general-purpose department?

  1. It doesn't; vice-chairs are ceremonial
  2. It writes science into the system's leadership: the pillar where research, monitoring, and early warning live is championed by the science department — giving science-based proposals an institutional advocate and a legible reporting line
  3. It means only DOST employees may do hazard research
  4. It transfers PAGASA to the military
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. Institutional design allocates advocacy: assigning the science department to lead the pre-disaster pillar is RA 10121's structural statement that risk reduction is a scientific enterprise. For a proposer it answers "who in government champions this work?" — the question every partnership paragraph must answer. A, C, D range from cynical to false.

Question 6 · Applying

Mid-fiscal-year, a strong typhoon strikes. The municipality's QRF is exhausted, but most of the 70% share remains. The DRRM officer asks what the law's structure implies. The best-grounded answer is:

  1. The 70% automatically converts to response money at first landfall
  2. The QRF is the standby response share; further response funding follows the augmentation and declaration mechanisms of the DRRM system, while the 70% remains programmed for risk reduction — the structure separates standby response from pre-disaster investment by design
  3. All DRRM spending must stop until next year
  4. The mayor may personally reallocate any fund by verbal order
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. The 30/70 architecture is deliberate: collapsing it at the first emergency would recreate the reactive regime the Act abolished, which is why extraordinary response needs run through the system's formal mechanisms (declarations, augmentation, higher-level funds) rather than silent raids on prevention money. A, C, D each break the structure in a different direction.

Question 7 · Analyzing

RA 10692's human-resources leg (training, scholarships, incentives for scientific personnel) previews a Week-10 theme. The structural point to carry forward is:

  1. Equipment alone modernizes an agency
  2. Modernization statutes treat people as infrastructure: instruments without trained, retained specialists deliver no better forecasts — the same logic the Magna Carta (RA 8439/11312) applies to S&T personnel across government
  3. Scholarships are prohibited in government
  4. Human resources are the finance department's concern only
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. The Act's three legs stand or fall together — a radar no one can maintain or interpret is a monument, not a modernization. Recognizing "people as statutory infrastructure" now will make the Magna Carta's benefits-and-retention architecture in Week 10 read as strategy rather than perks. A is the fallacy the Act legislates against.

Question 8 · Applying

Your observatory project wants eclipse timing predictions and historical ephemerides validated for Philippine coordinates. Which pairing of service line and statute correctly identifies the institutional source?

  1. Flood forecasting service line · RA 10121
  2. Astronomical services line of PAGASA · agency modernized under RA 10692
  3. Space industry development area · RA 10535
  4. Quick Response Fund · RA 11363
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. Ephemerides and eclipse predictions are the textbook content of PAGASA's astronomical services — the legacy line encoded in the agency's very name and carried through its modernization statute. The other options scramble statutes and functions; being able to un-scramble them quickly is precisely the case-recitation skill.

Question 9 · Evaluating

A network proposal budgets GPS-disciplined clocks for every station, citing RA 10535. A cost-cutter suggests deleting the line: "clocks are on every laptop." The strongest defense of the budget line is:

  1. Laptops lose time when unplugged
  2. The claim is metrological, not conversational: undisciplined system clocks drift by seconds, and the science (triangulation, simultaneous photometry) fails beyond fractions of a second; PhST-traceable discipline is what makes multi-station data one dataset — the statute supplies the national reference the design traces to
  3. RA 10535 legally requires GPS purchases
  4. Expensive equipment impresses reviewers
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. The defense must connect the line item to the design requirement (clock agreement at the tolerance the science needs) and to the standard being traced (PhST). C overclaims — the Act mandates synchronization duties for government offices, not equipment purchases by researchers; A is true but trivial; D is the budget-padding fallacy from Chapter 1's reviewer heuristic.

Question 10 · Applying

A radio station's evening news habitually announces a time about a minute fast. Under RA 10535's scheme, the correct account of the station's duty is:

  1. None — private media may broadcast any time they like, with no official-time regime touching them
  2. Official time announcements are part of the synchronization scheme: broadcast time signals should reflect PhST as kept by PAGASA, since disseminating a divergent "official" time defeats the single-timescale purpose of the Act
  3. The station must switch to international time zones
  4. The duty applies only during January
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. The dissemination chain (Figure 3.5) runs from the reference clock through government and broadcast announcements to the public precisely because a standard nobody hears is no standard. A misses the broadcast link in the statute's scheme; C invents a different regime; D repeats the Time-Consciousness-Week misreading.

Question 11 · Analyzing

Classify the four statutes by what they primarily hand a researcher (funds, institutions, standards). The correct triple-mapping is:

  1. 10121 → standards; 10535 → funds; 10692 & 11363 → nothing
  2. 10121 → funds & structures (LDRRMF, pillars); 10692 & 11363 → institutions (a modernized service agency; a space agency with programs); 10535 → standards (one legal timescale)
  3. All four → penalties only
  4. 10692 → time standard; 10535 → weather agency; the mapping is interchangeable
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. This is the chapter's key generalization: statutes hand you different classes of infrastructure — money, institutions, standards — and only one looks like money. Classifying a statute correctly tells you how to use it in a proposal: funds are partnered into, institutions are collaborated with, standards are designed to. A, C, D scramble or flatten the classes.

Question 12 · Applying

A student answer begins: "The complaint is invalid. Also, RA 10121 exists, and disasters are bad." In IRAC terms, the answer's chief structural defect is:

  1. The conclusion precedes any issue, rule, or application — a verdict without reasoning, followed by rule-adjacent filler that matches no facts to no elements
  2. It is too short to fail
  3. It cites the wrong Republic Act number
  4. Conclusions may never come first in any writing
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: A. IRAC's force is that the conclusion is earned by the preceding steps; a bare verdict plus vague gestures at the law earns no points because nothing can be checked. Note D's overreach: experienced writers sometimes state conclusions first (CRAC style) — the sin is absence of rule and application, not paragraph order.

Question 13 · Evaluating

Hypothetical: PhilSA proposes coordinating a national "dark-and-quiet skies" guideline to protect observation sites, working with LGUs. A critic objects that only environment agencies may touch such topics. The best evaluation under RA 11363's design is:

  1. The critic is right; space agencies handle rockets only
  2. The objection misreads the Act: space science and its enabling conditions sit within PhilSA's mandate, and its attachment to the Office of the President plus the Space Council exist precisely to coordinate cross-cutting matters — with environment agencies and LGUs as co-actors, not rivals
  3. Only Congress may discuss the night sky
  4. The guideline is invalid unless PAGASA objects
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. The Act's whole institutional logic — presidential attachment, a coordinating council, development areas spanning science, education, and cooperation — anticipates exactly such cross-cutting issues. The mature analysis assigns the convening role to the space institutions and the co-implementation to sectoral agencies. A, C, D shrink or scramble mandates.

Question 14 · Analyzing

Your case materials note the absence of a national astronomy and space science research roadmap. Chapter 3 adds the institutional layer. The most precise synthesis is:

  1. No institution could legally write such a roadmap
  2. The mandates already exist in fragments — PAGASA's astronomical services (RA 10692), PhilSA's space R&D and education areas (RA 11363), DOST's pillar science role (RA 10121) — so the roadmap's absence is a coordination gap between institutions, not a mandate gap in the law
  3. The roadmap exists but is classified
  4. Only the IAU may author national roadmaps
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. Reading the statutes together shows every ingredient present — service mandate, policy mandate, science leadership — distributed across agencies with no instrument yet binding them into one research agenda. Diagnosing the gap as coordination rather than authority sharpens the Chapter 2 essay's roadmap proposal: convening, not legislating, is the missing step. A, C, D evade the analysis.

Question 15 · Applying

A compliance paragraph in a proposal reads: "(R) RA 10535 requires government offices to synchronize with PhST; (A) all network stations, including those hosted in public schools, will run GPS-disciplined clocks traceable to PhST, checked monthly; (C) the project therefore meets and exceeds the synchronization standard." This paragraph demonstrates:

  1. A wasted paragraph — compliance is implied
  2. Compressed IRAC as proposal prose: obligation stated, workplan matched to its elements, conclusion drawn — the exam method reused as management writing
  3. Plagiarism of the statute
  4. An error, because schools are not government offices
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. This is Section 3.8's closing point made concrete: IRAC is a reasoning discipline, not an exam trick, and a compliance section is simply IRAC with the issue implicit. Quoting an obligation to comply with it is not plagiarism (C); and D quibbles the wrong way — public schools strengthen, not weaken, the synchronization argument.

Question 16 · Evaluating

Two project designs reach the same LGU: Design P trains the municipal DRRM office to run and maintain a flood-sensor network; Design Q installs the same network but keeps all operation with the university team "for data quality." Under RA 10121's decentralized structure, the stronger design is:

  1. Q — universities are permanent, LGUs are not
  2. P — the Act builds DRRM capacity at every level down to the barangay, so embedding operation in the LDRRMO matches the statute's architecture, sustains the network beyond the grant, and turns the LGU from site into stakeholder; data-quality concerns are met by protocols and audits, not by monopoly
  3. Q — data quality overrides all other considerations
  4. Neither; sensors are response-pillar items
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. Statute-aware design aligns with the law's institutional grain: RA 10121 deliberately localizes DRRM capacity, so a project that leaves capability behind fits the system and survives project end. The data-quality objection is real but solvable inside P (SOPs, calibration schedules, joint QA) — a classic internal-vs-external validity style trade-off resolved by design rather than by exclusion.

Question 17 · Analyzing

Why does this chapter insist you identify, for each statute, its definitions section before answering any case question about it?

  1. Definitions sections are the shortest part
  2. Because statutory terms mean what the statute says they mean — not what everyday usage suggests — and most case disputes turn on whether the facts fit a defined term's elements (who counts as covered personnel, what counts as a "space activity," which fund qualifies)
  3. Because graders check word counts
  4. Definitions only matter to lawyers, not researchers
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. The definitions section is the rulebook for every other section: in the Application step, elements come from definitions, and next week's Magna Carta cases (who qualifies as S&T personnel) will turn on exactly this. Everyday meanings are the single most common source of confidently wrong case answers.

Question 18 · Applying

A team plans a high-school "space week" with three components: a timekeeping-history exhibit, a typhoon-tracking workshop using real archived tracks, and a satellite-imagery mapping exercise. Matched to statutory hooks, the most accurate mapping is:

  1. Exhibit → RA 10535 (time consciousness); workshop → RA 10692 (PAGASA data/services); mapping exercise → RA 11363 (space education & awareness)
  2. All three → RA 10121 exclusively
  3. Exhibit → RA 11363; workshop → RA 10535; mapping → RA 10692
  4. Statutory hooks are unnecessary for school events
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: A. Each component matches the statute whose purpose it genuinely serves — the honesty test running through this course: timekeeping content serves the time-consciousness purpose, archived typhoon tracks draw on PAGASA's service mandate, and satellite-imagery education sits squarely in the Space Act's education area. C shuffles the hooks into mismatches; B flattens them; D forfeits free institutional legitimacy (and partners).

Question 19 · Evaluating

A senior researcher scoffs: "Reading laws is the admin staff's job; scientists should read papers." The strongest rebuttal from this chapter's framework is:

  1. Admin staff dislike reading laws too
  2. Statutes are terrain: they decide who must help you (mandates), what money exists (funds), what your instruments must trace to (standards), and what can stop your fieldwork (constraints) — a PI who outsources that knowledge outsources project design itself
  3. Scientists who read laws become lawyers
  4. Papers cite laws occasionally, so reading papers suffices
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. The mandates/money/standards/constraints framework is a project-design tool, not legal scholarship: every quadrant changes what the workplan says (partners, budget lines, QA, compliance). Delegating it wholesale means designing blind. A is commiseration, C a non sequitur, D mistakes secondhand fragments for reconnaissance.

Question 20 · Creating / Evaluating

You must draft ONE sentence for a proposal's rationale that correctly stacks three layers — statute, institution, and standard — from this chapter. Which sentence does it?

  1. "Space is important to laws, institutions, and standards."
  2. "Anchored in the space R&D and education areas declared by RA 11363, the project will operate in coordination with PhilSA and PAGASA's astronomical services, with all station timestamps disciplined to Philippine Standard Time under RA 10535."
  3. "This project complies with all Philippine laws, whichever they are."
  4. "RA 10121, RA 10692, RA 10535, and RA 11363 are hereby cited."
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. One sentence, three verifiable layers: a named statutory anchor (policy areas of RA 11363), named institutional partners matched to their actual mandates (PhilSA; PAGASA's astronomical services), and a named standard with its statute (PhST, RA 10535). A and C are content-free; D is citation-dumping — the statutory version of the agenda-washing this course has now taught you to detect in two different domains.

II.Reinforcement Essay — 3 HOTS Items

Write each essay in full (300–500 words) before opening the dropdown — Essays 1 and 2 must follow the IRAC structure explicitly. The model answers are possible strong responses; yours may differ and still earn full marks if it demonstrates the same statutory reasoning identified in the explanation.

Essay 1 · Evaluating (IRAC) · 20 points

Typhoon-battered Municipality X passes an ordinance directing that, "for the next three years, 100% of the LDRRMF shall be pre-committed to relief goods, evacuation logistics, and post-typhoon cash aid, response being the most urgent need of our people." A university team's proposal to co-fund a flood-sensor and rain-gauge network from the fund is denied, citing the ordinance. Using full IRAC, evaluate the ordinance's validity and the denial, and advise the team on its best lawful path to partnership.

Show possible correct answer & explanation

Model answer (outline of a full-credit response). Issue: May an LGU, by ordinance, pre-commit the entire LDRRMF to response-side expenditure for multiple years — and may it deny prevention/mitigation programming on that basis? Rule: RA 10121 requires LGUs to set aside not less than 5% of estimated revenue from regular sources as the LDRRMF; of this, 30% stands as a Quick Response Fund, while the remaining 70% supports disaster prevention, mitigation, and preparedness activities consistent with the local DRRM plan; unexpended balances accrue to a special trust fund for DRRM use within five years. The Act's declaration of policy adopts the proactive risk-reduction paradigm. A local ordinance, being subordinate legislation, cannot override a statute. Application: The ordinance's 100% response pre-commitment collapses the statutory 30/70 architecture — it does not merely reallocate within discretion but abolishes the prevention/mitigation share the statute reserves. Element by element: (1) the fund exists by statute, not by council grace; (2) the 70% category is defined by purpose (pre-disaster risk reduction), which relief goods and cash aid do not satisfy; (3) the multi-year pre-commitment also defeats the trust-fund mechanism by consuming balances the law preserves for DRRM across years; (4) the sensor-network proposal is squarely a prevention/preparedness activity — the category the ordinance unlawfully zeroed out. The denial therefore rests on an invalid basis. Conclusion: The ordinance is invalid to the extent it devotes the entire LDRRMF to response; the denial grounded on it is likewise infirm. Advice: rather than litigate, the team should (a) present the sensor network for inclusion in the local DRRM plan as a 70%-share prevention/preparedness activity, (b) brief the LDRRMC on the 30/70 structure using the primary text, and (c) offer the MOU-based partnership model — LGU as owner-operator, university as designer-trainer — which matches both the statute's localization design and the LGU's urgency narrative (a working early-warning network is response-relevant).

Why this earns full marks. All four IRAC steps are explicit, and the Application does element-by-element work (fund source, purpose categories, trust-fund mechanism, ordinance-versus-statute hierarchy) instead of leaping from rule to verdict. The answer also does what professional advice does: separates invalidity analysis from the pragmatic path (plan inclusion, education, MOU). Answers that declare the ordinance "wrong" without walking the elements, or that miss the ordinance/statute hierarchy point, land at proficient; missing the 30/70 structure entirely fails the item's core.

Essay 2 · Evaluating (IRAC) · 20 points

A national scientific facility timestamps its public datasets using a server clock that has drifted 47 seconds from PhST; when users complain that its transit-timing data cannot be merged with other stations', the facility answers that "RA 10535 is about office punctuality, not servers." Using full IRAC, analyze the facility's position, then explain — beyond bare compliance — why a research facility should treat time-traceability as a scientific obligation, citing the metrological logic of this chapter.

Show possible correct answer & explanation

Model answer (outline of a full-credit response). Issue: Does a government scientific facility's duty under RA 10535 extend to the machine clocks that timestamp its official data outputs — and is "the law is about punctuality" a tenable reading? Rule: RA 10535 establishes Philippine Standard Time as the single official time, designates PAGASA as official timekeeper, and requires government offices to synchronize their timepieces with PhST; the Act's purpose is a uniform national timescale, disseminated through official channels. Application: (1) The facility is a government office; its duty to synchronize is not disputed. (2) "Timepieces" read purposively covers the instruments by which the office keeps and publishes time — in a data facility, that is the server clock stamping official outputs; a wall clock reading that differs from the data clock would satisfy the statute's letter while defeating its purpose. (3) The punctuality framing confuses the Act's awareness campaign (Time Consciousness Week) with its operative core (one legal timescale, official keeper, synchronization duties). (4) The 47-second drift is precisely the harm the single-timescale design prevents: the facility is disseminating a divergent de facto time to every downstream user. Conclusion: The facility's position fails; it should discipline its data clocks to PhST (NTP chain or GPS-disciplined source traceable to the official timekeeper) and state the traceability in its data documentation. Beyond compliance: metrologically, a timestamp is a measurement, and measurements need traceability to a reference — the same logic as photometric calibration. Untraceable time converts a facility's data from evidence into anecdote: transit timings cannot be merged, cross-station events cannot be identified as the same event, and reliability (consistency) is achieved without validity (accuracy) — the Chapter 1 asymmetry reborn as a clock. A facility that publishes data has therefore assumed a scientific duty of traceability that would exist even if RA 10535 did not.

Why this earns full marks. The IRAC is complete and the Application confronts the facility's actual argument (letter-versus-purpose; campaign-versus-core) rather than a strawman. The discriminator is the final movement: connecting statutory synchronization to measurement traceability, and reusing the reliability/validity asymmetry from Chapter 1 — evidence the student is integrating the course, not reciting the week. Answers that stop at "the law says synchronize" earn proficient; answers that miss the government-office status or read the statute as punctuality-only fail the analysis.

Essay 3 · Creating · 20 points

Design a two-year "Provincial Sky and Storm Observatory" program — one integrated facility hosting a meteor/fireball camera network, an automated weather station cluster, and a public astronomy education hall. Write its institutional and legal architecture: (i) for each of this chapter's four statutes, state the specific mandate, fund, standard, or constraint your design draws on and what the statute receives in return; (ii) name the coordination instruments (MOUs, plan inclusions, letters) your workplan needs and with whom; and (iii) close with the two most likely institutional risks and your mitigation for each.

Show possible correct answer & explanation

Model answer (outline of a full-credit response). (i) Four-statute architecture. RA 10121: draws on the prevention/mitigation pillar (DOST-championed) and partnership access to the provincial LDRRMF's 70% share for the weather-station cluster and its maintenance training; returns to the DRRM system a real-time local observation feed and a trained PDRRMO unit — inclusion in the Provincial DRRM Plan is the entry instrument. RA 10692: draws on PAGASA's mandate twice — archived climatological data to site and calibrate the stations, and the astronomical services line for ephemerides and eclipse/meteor-shower programming in the education hall; returns station data to densify PAGASA's observation network and a provincial platform for its warnings — a data-sharing and calibration MOU is the instrument. RA 10535: supplies the standard: all cameras and AWS log in PhST-traceable time via GPS-disciplined clocks (budget line), making the meteor triangulation and storm timing mergeable with national datasets; the education hall returns a permanent "how astronomers built the clock" exhibit anchored to National Time Consciousness Week each January. RA 11363: supplies the policy anchor (space R&D; space education and awareness) and the program door — PhilSA cooperation for imagery, curriculum materials, and scholarship pathways for staff; returns a functioning provincial node for space education and a documented model other provinces can replicate. (ii) Coordination instruments: MOU with PAGASA (data, calibration, ephemerides); inclusion in the Provincial DRRM Plan via PDRRMC resolution (fund access, sustainability); letter-of-cooperation or program agreement with PhilSA (education content, replication); host agreement with the provincial government and DepEd division (site, staffing, school visits); all executed before Month 6, sequenced in the Gantt chart. (iii) Risks and mitigations: Risk 1 — mandate friction (PAGASA and PhilSA both plausibly "own" parts of the program): mitigated by writing both into the design with distinct, mandate-matched roles (services and data vs. policy and education) and a joint steering committee — coordination by architecture, not by hope. Risk 2 — post-grant abandonment (the classic fate of donated instruments): mitigated by the RA 10121 route — LDRRMO operation and LDRRMF-programmed maintenance make the province the owner-operator with a statutory funding line, while the university retains a QA/audit role via the MOU.

Why this earns full marks. The design treats each statute as a different class of infrastructure (fund/structure, institution/data, standard, policy/program door) and — the discriminator — specifies what flows back to each institution, converting stakeholders into beneficiaries. Coordination is instantiated in named instruments with timing, not asserted as "we will coordinate." The risk section reuses the chapter's two signature warnings (overlap-by-silence; sustainability through the statute's own localization design). Answers that map the statutes correctly but skip the reciprocity, the named instruments, or the risk mitigation are proficient; answers that treat all four statutes as funding sources repeat the flattening error the framework exists to prevent.
