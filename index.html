Course Description

The primary aim of this course is for astronomy students to experience real-life research writing of grant proposals. It exposes students to research and development (R&D) management with the application of science and technology (S&T) in the context of astronomy for sustainable economic growth and development. The course also surveys various Philippine laws relevant to astronomy and space science, providing a more comprehensive perspective on developing a research project.

The course facilitates students’ skills in R&D productivity, promotion, technology transfer and utilization, S&T human resources development, astronomical science literacy, and advocacy — and builds the capacity to establish linkages by creating and promoting appropriate technologies in the field of astronomy and space science.

Final requirement. At the end of this course, students should be able to produce a complete R&D proposal appropriate for a research funding application, culminating in a presentation of the research agenda and an innovative pitching session evaluated with a standard rubric. Enhanced

Learning Tools Required

Since this course deals with research proposal writing for a funding application, students will need a computer, laptop, or smartphone with at least the following: a word processor (MS Word 2007+ or Google Docs), a spreadsheet application (MS Excel 2007+ or Google Sheets), or the WPS Office suite; presentation software for the pitching session; and a stable internet connection for e-RTU Flexys and class correspondence. VI.Course Requirements and Grading System

Course Requirements / Outputs

  • Assessments (Assessments 01–07, quizzes, and asynchronous tasks)
  • Class reinforcements — research attitude, case recitation, and participation in workshops and dry runs
  • Case Analysis — group written analysis (maximum of 5 members) of legal-scenario cases on RA 8439, RA 11312, RA 10055, DOST AO 001 s. 2020, DOST AO 508 s. 2013, and DOST SO 009 s. 2021 New
  • Major examinations / project outputs — Long Quiz 1, Midterm Examination, and the Final Examination (presentation of research agenda)
  • Complete R&D proposal appropriate for a research funding application, with detailed workplan and line-item budget

Innovative Pitching Rubric New

The final pitching session is scored on nine criteria, each rated on a four-point scale: Excellent (4) — outstanding mastery and integration of science and community application; Proficient (3) — solid understanding with minor gaps; Developing (2) — partial understanding, needs improvement; Beginning (1) — minimal understanding or application.

CriterionWhat the panel looks for at the “Excellent” level
Problem Identification & Community RelevanceA specific, persistent community problem supported by data or evidence, with strong justification of urgency and relevance
Integration of Astronomy ConceptsAccurate and innovative use of astronomy (e.g., celestial mechanics, solar activity, satellite orbits) directly linked to the solution
Scientific Soundness & FeasibilityA scientifically valid, realistic solution that is feasible within community constraints
Innovation & CreativityA highly original and creative approach to solving the problem
Societal Impact & SustainabilityClearly articulated long-term impact, sustainability, and benefits to the community
Economic or Value-Creation PotentialA clear explanation of how the idea can generate value, savings, or revenue
Clarity of Pitch & CommunicationA clear, logical, engaging, well-structured pitch with effective visuals or data
Use of Evidence & DataStrong use of data, references, or empirical evidence to support claims
Ethical, Environmental, and Policy AwarenessStrong awareness of ethical, environmental, and policy implications

Total score interpretation: 90–100% Highly Innovative & Community-Ready Proposal · 75–89% Strong Proposal with Refinement Needed · 60–74% Conceptual but Requires Major Improvement · Below 60% Not Yet Ready for Implementation.

VIII.Course Policies

Schedule

This course is available for live virtual class meetings for discussions, consultations, and other interactive sessions during asynchronous periods, in addition to the scheduled face-to-face laboratory sessions.

General Policy

  1. This course strictly follows the university code/manual for student academics and proper behavior.
  2. No one is allowed to humiliate, in any form, anyone in the class.
  3. The class strictly implements a similarity index and originality standard for papers. A plagiarism checker will be applied to submitted work. Students who show dishonesty toward scholarly works will be dealt with accordingly, based on university code rules and policies.

Academic Integrity and Responsible Use of AI Tools New

  1. Generative AI tools may be used only as assistive aids — for example, brainstorming, grammar checking, or clarifying legal texts — and never to produce submitted work wholesale. All submissions must reflect the student’s own analysis and writing.
  2. Any use of AI tools in an output must be disclosed in a brief note at the end of the submission (tool used and purpose).
  3. Undisclosed or wholesale AI-generated work is treated as a violation of the originality policy above, alongside plagiarism.

Class Policy (for strict implementation)

  1. All correspondence in this class will be sent to this email: rmguido@rtu.edu.ph.
  2. Students are highly encouraged to use the @rtu.edu.ph email account. If you do not have this email, kindly request one from the Management Information Center (MIC). The RTU account gives free access to Microsoft applications.
  3. For all correspondence/submissions, follow the subject-heading format TE01_<SURNAME>_<SECTION CODE>_<DOCUMENT>. Examples: TE01_GUIDO_CEIT-34-301A_ASSESSMENT01; TE01_GUIDO_CEIT-34-301A_RESEARCH PROPOSAL DRAFT; TE01_GUIDO_CEIT-34-301A_RESEARCH PROPOSAL PPT.
  4. Lectures can be found on e-RTU Flexys or in the Facebook Units (you will be invited).
  5. You may use the Facebook Units for student discussions and quick questions or clarifications.
  6. The professor is open to scheduling a virtual live meeting with individuals or teams, by request, at pre-arranged schedules.
  7. Show courtesy and proper professionalism when emailing documents. To build your early scientific and research career and scholarly stature, configure a professional email signature (name, program, department, university, contact details).
  8. For questions and consultations, kindly allow a 24–48 hour response time. Please understand that other equally important matters are being handled besides this class.
  9. Expect additional supplemental materials and readings.

Online Meetings
