3.1Why Scientists Must Read Statutes: Law as Research Infrastructure

To an astronomy project manager, a law is three things at once: a mandate (someone is legally required to do something you may need), a fund (money is appropriated or required to flow somewhere), and a constraint (your project must comply with it). Reading statutes is therefore not a legal nicety — it is reconnaissance of the terrain your project will operate on.

Philippine legal instruments form a hierarchy, and knowing which layer you are quoting determines how strong your citation is and what can override what:

How a Republic Act Is Born — and How to Read One Correctly

A Republic Act is the surviving output of a long filter: a bill filed in the House or Senate, committee hearings (where agencies and stakeholders — including, sometimes, scientists — put evidence on the record), three readings in each chamber, a bicameral conference committee reconciling the two versions, and the President's signature (or an unvetoed lapse into law; a veto can be overridden by two-thirds of both chambers). The Act then takes effect after publication in the Official Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation. Two research-relevant facts fall out of this process: first, the committee records and explanatory notes behind a statute are readable evidence of what problem Congress thought it was solving — useful context when a provision seems ambiguous; second, the implementing agency's IRR follows enactment, sometimes slowly, which is why a young statute may be law without yet being fully operable procedure.

When you interpret statutory text in a case answer, apply the standard canons in order: the plain-meaning rule (words are read in their ordinary sense unless the statute defines them); statutory definitions control (Section 3 of most acts overrides the dictionary — the single most decisive rule for your Case Analysis); the specific governs the general (a provision about S&T personnel beats a general civil-service rule on the same point); and the later law prevails over an earlier inconsistent one (which is how RA 11312 amends RA 8439 in Week 10's material). These four canons, plus the hierarchy of Figure 3.1, resolve the great majority of scenario questions this course will throw at you.

Key Concept
Cite the primary text — the RA or IRR itself from the Official Gazette or an authoritative repository — never a news summary or blog paraphrase. In your Case Analysis, answers must quote the section: "Based on Section 2 of RA 11312, it stipulates that…"
Common Pitfall
Treating IRRs as optional reading. The RA gives you the principle; the IRR gives you the procedure — application forms, deadlines, committee compositions. A workplan that cites RA 11363 but ignores its IRR will misstate how anything actually gets done.

3.3RA 10121 (2010): The Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act

RA 10121 is the single clearest example in Philippine law of a paradigm shift written into statute: it moved the country from a reactive, response-centered disaster regime to a proactive, science-based risk reduction and management regime.

Before 2010, disaster governance ran on a civil-defense logic — wait, respond, relieve. RA 10121 replaced it with the international DRRM paradigm: risk is the product of hazard, exposure, and vulnerability, and public money is better spent reducing risk before disasters than compensating losses after. The Act restructured governance around the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC) — chaired by the Secretary of National Defense, with the Office of Civil Defense (OCD) as executive arm — and organized all DRRM work into four thematic pillars, each with a vice-chair agency:

Two features matter enormously for researchers. First, DOST vice-chairs the Prevention and Mitigation pillar — hazard science, monitoring networks, and early-warning R&D are not merely welcome in the DRRM system; they are its statutory foundation. Second, the Local DRRM Fund exists in every LGU: at least 5% of estimated revenue from regular sources, of which 30% is a standby Quick Response Fund and 70% supports prevention, mitigation, and preparedness activities — a category that can host research, hazard mapping, and monitoring instrumentation at the local level.

Definitions, Plans, and the State of Calamity

RA 10121's definitions section is a small course in risk science: it defines hazard (the potentially damaging event or phenomenon), exposure (people and assets in harm's way), vulnerability (the conditions that make them susceptible), and capacity (the resources available to cope) — and then defines DRRM itself as the systematic process of reducing the risk those factors compose. Because the statute adopts the scientific vocabulary wholesale, a hazard-mapping proposal can quote the Act's own definitions as its conceptual framework: law and methodology speak one language here. The lineage is international — the Act carried the Hyogo Framework's paradigm into Philippine law, and Philippine practice now tracks its successor, the Sendai Framework 2015–2030, with its emphasis on understanding risk (priority one: effectively, a research mandate).

The structure is deliberately deep as well as wide. Beneath the NDRRMC sit regional councils, then provincial, city, and municipal DRRM councils, and in every LGU a standing local DRRM office (with barangay committees at the base) — a permanent bureaucracy for risk, not an ad-hoc typhoon committee, and the natural institutional partner Figure 3.7 maps for you. Plans mirror the councils: a national DRRM framework and plan cascade into local DRRM plans, which is where the LDRRMF's 70% is programmed. Finally, the Act governs the declaration of a state of calamity — a legal switch that, once thrown by the President or the local sanggunian on the council's recommendation, activates consequences with real economic force: price controls on basic necessities, expedited access to calamity funds, and related emergency measures. For researchers the declaration matters twice — it is the legal environment your field team may operate in after a disaster, and it is the downstream event your early-warning science exists to make less costly.

Astronomy in Focus
Space weather is DRRM: geomagnetic storms disrupt GNSS positioning, HF communications, and power systems. An ionospheric-scintillation monitoring proposal is legible under RA 10121 as prevention-and-mitigation science — with DOST as pillar vice-chair and LGU/national DRRM structures as institutional users (recall the DRR-CCA door of the HNRDA, Figure 2.6).
For the Proposal
If your project produces hazard-relevant information, name its place in the four-pillar framework and identify the DRRM office (national OCD or the local DRRMO) that would consume the output. "Early warning" claims belong in Prevention/Mitigation and Preparedness — not Response.
Common Pitfall
Citing the LDRRMF as if it were a research grant window. It is an LGU-managed public fund with its own planning process (the LDRRM Plan); a researcher accesses it through partnership with the LGU, not by application to it.

3.4RA 10692 (2015): The PAGASA Modernization Act

PAGASA — the Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration, an agency under DOST — carries a mandate far broader than "weather bureau": weather forecasting and typhoon warning, flood forecasting, climate monitoring and climatology, and astronomical services, the legacy service line that long made PAGASA the State's institutional home for astronomy.

RA 10692 confronted a hard fact: forecasting quality is capped by instrumentation and people. The Act mandates a modernization program for PAGASA built on three legs:

  • Equipment and infrastructure — upgrading observation networks (radars, automated weather stations, upper-air facilities) and forecasting systems;
  • Human resources — training, scholarships, and incentives to develop and retain scientific personnel (a preview of the Magna Carta themes of Week 10); and
  • Service delivery — timely, understandable, actionable warnings reaching LGUs and communities.

From the Observatorio to PAGASA: A 160-Year Lineage

The agency's history is one of the deepest scientific lineages in Asia. The Jesuit Observatorio de Manila, founded in 1865, made the Philippines a regional pioneer of atmospheric science — Fr. Federico Faura's typhoon warnings in 1879 were among the first systematic storm warnings in the region, and the Observatorio's work spanned meteorology, seismology, geomagnetism, and astronomy. The American administration built the government Weather Bureau (1901) on that foundation; the 1972 government reorganization created PAGASA in its modern form under what became DOST. The astronomical services line is therefore not an ornament appended to a weather bureau — it is the continuous thread of the institution's origin, carried today through the agency's planetarium and astronomical publications, its ephemerides and eclipse and Ramadan crescent-visibility computations, its public observatory work, and its custody of Philippine Standard Time (the bridge to RA 10535 in the next section).

Read against that history, RA 10692's three modernization legs acquire specific content. Equipment means the observation network a forecast actually stands on: Doppler weather radars ringing the archipelago, automated weather and rain gauges densifying the surface network, upper-air stations, flood forecasting and warning systems for the major river basins, and storm-surge and numerical-prediction capability. Human resources means the meteorologist pipeline — structured training, scholarships for atmospheric science degrees, and the incentives needed to keep specialists whom foreign services and airlines actively recruit (the retention problem the Magna Carta of Week 10 attacks economy-wide). Service delivery means the last mile: impact-based forecasts and color-coded rainfall warnings that an LDRRMO can act on within the RA 10121 machinery you just studied — the two statutes are one system viewed from the science side and the response side.

PH Context
For decades before PhilSA existed, PAGASA's astronomical services — the planetarium, ephemerides, eclipse predictions, timekeeping — were the Philippine government's only standing astronomy function. Institutional memory, data archives, and public trust accumulated there; a wise proposal treats PAGASA as a partner, not a predecessor.
For the Proposal
Need historical typhoon tracks, rainfall series, or astronomical ephemerides? PAGASA's service mandate is your access argument. Write the data request into your workplan with lead time, and cite the specific service line you are drawing on.

3.5RA 10535 (2013): The Philippine Standard Time Act

RA 10535 looks like the smallest law in this chapter and is in fact the purest example of law as scientific infrastructure: it legislates a single national timescale.

The Act establishes Philippine Standard Time (PhST) as the official time of the country, designates PAGASA as the official timekeeper, and requires government offices and official broadcasts to synchronize with PhST. It institutionalizes time discipline culturally as well: the first week of January is National Time Consciousness Week, a statutory campaign against the habit politely called "Filipino time."

The Metrology Behind the Statute

What PAGASA keeps when it keeps PhST is a chain of traceability. The world's civil time, Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), is computed by international agreement from an ensemble of atomic clocks — devices that define the second by the resonance of cesium atoms, stable to parts in billions — with leap seconds occasionally inserted to keep atomic time within a second of the Earth's slightly irregular rotation (a reconciliation between the astronomer's day and the physicist's second). Philippine Standard Time is UTC+8, held year-round — the statute contemplates no daylight saving — and the official timekeeper's job is to realize that timescale locally and disseminate it: through announcements and synchronization services down the chain of Figure 3.5, and in practice through the network protocols (NTP servers, GPS-disciplined references) by which machines inherit time from standards. When your workplan budgets a GPS-disciplined clock, it is buying a private branch of this same traceability tree.

The statute's duty side is administrative rather than dramatic: officials responsible for timekeeping in government offices must keep official timepieces synchronized, and official broadcasts carry the standard outward; National Time Consciousness Week each January is the cultural campaign bolted onto the technical core. The deeper design lesson generalizes far beyond clocks — standards statutes work by making one reference authoritative and then wiring every public-facing instrument to it. You will meet the same architecture in calibration hierarchies, geodetic datums (the reference frames beneath the baselines of Chapter 4), and data standards; recognize it once here and you will recognize it everywhere.

Astronomy in Focus
Astronomy is the historical parent of timekeeping — and its hungriest consumer. A two-station meteor triangulation network with a 0.5-second clock disagreement produces trajectory errors of tens of kilometers; occultation timing is worthless without a traceable timescale. When your TE01 workplan includes distributed instruments, time synchronization is a line item (GPS-disciplined clocks, NTP servers) — RA 10535 gives you the national reference to trace to.
Key Concept
The pattern to generalize: some statutes hand you funds (RA 10121), some hand you institutions (RA 10692, RA 11363), and some hand you standards (RA 10535). All three are research infrastructure; only the first looks like money.

3.6RA 11363 (2019) and Its IRR: The Philippine Space Act

Chapter 2 read RA 11363 as an alignment anchor; now we read it as an institutional design. The Act declares the Philippine Space Policy around six key development areas (Figure 2.8), and then builds the machinery to pursue them:

  • The Philippine Space Agency (PhilSA) — the central government agency for space science, technology, and applications, attached to the Office of the President for whole-of-government reach, headed by a Director General;
  • The Philippine Space Council — the high-level advisory and coordinating body that keeps space policy coherent across departments;
  • A Philippine Space Development Fund and regular appropriations to finance programs; and
  • The IRR of RA 11363, which operationalizes all of the above — organizational details, transition of existing space programs, and procedures.

PhilSA's Functions and the Treaty Layer Above the Statute

Read PhilSA's statutory functions as a menu of what a central space agency does: it advises on and formulates space policy and the country's space development plans; it runs programs across the six development areas — satellite missions and their ground segments, space data services for agriculture, environment, and disaster response, scholarships and human-capital pipelines, and education and awareness campaigns; it represents the Philippines in international space fora and cooperation agreements; and it carries the regulatory and registry side of spacefaring — maintaining the national registry of Philippine space objects and overseeing the conduct of space activities. The agency also inherited and continues the pre-PhilSA satellite lineage: the Diwata microsatellites and Maya cubesats built through earlier DOST-university programs proved the human capital that the Space Act then institutionalized — a sequence worth citing whenever someone claims a developing country cannot grow space capability from universities.

Above the statute sits a treaty layer that explains several of its choices. The Outer Space Treaty (1967) — space law's constitution — establishes that outer space is free for exploration and use by all States, not subject to national appropriation, reserved for peaceful purposes; that States bear international responsibility for national space activities, including those of private actors, and liability for damage their space objects cause; and companion agreements (notably the Registration Convention) require States to register space objects. This is why RA 11363 builds registration and supervision machinery: a State that launches — or authorizes anyone to launch — answers for it internationally. For a proposal, the treaty layer is one more anchor altitude: statute for the national mandate, treaty for the international one — the same layered-citation habit as SDG target plus PDP strategy in Chapter 2.

Key Concept
Attachment matters. PhilSA sits with the Office of the President rather than inside one line department — the legal signal that space policy must coordinate across defense, science, agriculture, education, and communications, not compete within one of them. When you propose multi-agency work, this is the coordination spine you invoke.
PH Context
Recall the standing gap from your case materials: RA 11363 names space R&D and space education as policy areas, yet no dedicated national research roadmap for astronomy and space science exists. The statute supplies the mandate for exactly the roadmap your Week 14–17 research agenda can draft — the strongest kind of "gap + mandate" rationale.

3.7From Statute to Opportunity: The Researcher's Mapping Framework

The skill this chapter builds is a repeatable mapping: for any statute, extract the mandates, money, standards, and constraints relevant to your project — then write them into the proposal as partners, funds, compliance items, and rationale.

Run the mapping across this chapter's four laws for a single example project — a provincial meteor-camera and fireball-alert network: RA 10121 gives it a user (the LDRRMO) and a pillar (prevention/mitigation); RA 10692 gives it a partner and data source (PAGASA stations, sky archives); RA 10535 gives it a standard (PhST-disciplined timestamps across stations); RA 11363 gives it a policy anchor and program door (space R&D and education areas, PhilSA cooperation). One project, four statutes, no lawyer required.

Common Pitfall
Institutional overlap handled by silence. Astronomy touches both PAGASA (astronomical services, RA 10692) and PhilSA (space science, RA 11363). A proposal that names only one — or worse, neither — invites the panel question "have you coordinated with…?" Answer it in advance with an explicit coordination paragraph or a letter of support from each.

3.8The IRAC Method: Answering Case Questions Like a Professional

Your Case Recitation (this week) and group Case Analysis (Week 10) grade one skill above all: disciplined legal reasoning on a factual scenario. The professional framework for it is IRAC — Issue, Rule, Application, Conclusion.

Worked micro-example with this chapter's material: An LGU spends its entire LDRRMF on relief goods stockpiles, arguing "disasters are unpredictable." I: May the whole LDRRMF be devoted to response items? R: RA 10121 structures the fund as 30% QRF for response, with 70% supporting prevention, mitigation, and preparedness. A: Stockpiles are response-side expenditure; devoting 100% collapses the statutory 70/30 structure and defeats the Act's proactive paradigm — the very shift the law was written to make. C: Therefore the allocation is improper; at most the QRF share may sit as response standby, and the LGU should program the 70% toward risk-reduction activities — which can legitimately include hazard research and monitoring.

Variants, Grading, and a Second Worked Example

IRAC has professional dialects. CRAC/CREAC front-loads the conclusion (Conclusion–Rule–Explanation–Application–Conclusion) — the memo style of practicing lawyers, acceptable in your course answers so long as the rule and application still appear in full; the sin is never the order, always the missing middle. In grading terms, expect the weight to sit where the reasoning sits: a sliver for framing the issue, a solid share for the precise rule with its section citation, the largest share for the element-by-element application, and the remainder for a conclusion that answers the exact question asked (including, where the facts demand it, "it depends — on this specific missing fact"). Answers that are all rule are book reports; answers that are all conclusion are opinions; the middle is where the profession lives.

A second worked micro-example, this time from RA 10535: A government research institute's IT officer refuses to synchronize the institute's servers with PhST, arguing the machines run "an internal research network, not official clocks." I: Does the synchronization duty reach a government institute's data-timestamping servers? R: RA 10535 establishes PhST as the single official time, kept by PAGASA, with which government offices must synchronize their timepieces. A: The institute is a government office; the servers stamp its official outputs, making them timepieces in the sense that serves the statute's purpose — a single national timescale would be defeated if an office's wall clock complied while its published data dissented; the "internal network" label does not change the data's public, official character. C: The refusal is unfounded; the servers should be disciplined to PhST-traceable time. Notice the move that scored: the Application confronted the officer's actual argument (the internal/official distinction) rather than restating the rule louder — the same purposive reading you will need when definitions are contested in the Magna Carta cases.

Key Concept
In case answers, the definitions section and the numbers are your evidence: 5%, 70/30, five-year trust fund, "official timekeeper," "attached to the Office of the President." Vague answers cite the law's spirit; scoring answers cite its sections.
For the Proposal
IRAC is not only for exams: an entire compliance paragraph of your final proposal can be written as compressed IRAC — the obligation (R), how your workplan meets it (A), and the conclusion that the project is compliant (C).

Question 1 · Analyzing

An IRR provision imposes a requirement that directly contradicts the text of the Republic Act it implements. Applying the hierarchy of legal instruments, the correct resolution is:

  1. The IRR prevails because it is more recent and more detailed
  2. The RA prevails: implementing rules must stay within statutory bounds, and an IRR provision inconsistent with its statute is invalid to the extent of the inconsistency
  3. Both are void, and Congress must re-enact the law
  4. The agency may choose whichever is more convenient
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. IRRs exist to operationalize statutes — procedures, timelines, details — under authority delegated by the statute itself; that delegation cannot be used to expand or contradict the delegating law. Recency and detail (A) do not outrank hierarchy; C overreacts (only the inconsistent provision fails); D describes no legal system at all.

Question 2 · Evaluating

For a Case Analysis answer on RA 10121, Student X cites a news article summarizing the law; Student Y quotes Section 21 on the LDRRMF from the Official Gazette text. Y's answer is stronger chiefly because:

  1. News articles are always wrong
  2. Legal reasoning must rest on the primary text — the rule as enacted — since summaries paraphrase, omit elements, and cannot be matched fact-to-element in the Application step
  3. The Official Gazette is easier to read
  4. Section numbers impress graders regardless of content
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. IRAC's Rule step requires the governing provision itself — "Based on Section __ of RA ____, it stipulates that…" — because Application means matching facts against the rule's precise elements (percentages, definitions, conditions). A paraphrase cannot supply those elements, however accurate it happens to be. A is false as a universal; C and D are cosmetic.

PhilSA is attached to the Office of the President rather than housed inside a line department. The chapter's explanation of why this design matters is:

  1. Office space is cheaper in Malacañang
  2. Space policy cuts across defense, science, agriculture, education, and communications; attachment to the presidency signals whole-of-government coordination rather than competition inside one department's budget and priorities
  3. It exempts PhilSA from all laws
  4. It makes the Director General the president's spokesperson
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. Institutional placement is policy: an agency inside one department inherits that department's lens, while attachment to the presidency positions it to coordinate across all of them — the same logic as the Philippine Space Council's high-level composition. For proposers, it is the coordination spine to invoke for multi-agency projects. A, C, D are distractors of varying absurdity.

Question 14 · Analyzing

Distinguish the Philippine Space Council from PhilSA. The correct division of labor is:

  1. The Council implements missions; PhilSA advises the President
  2. The Council is the high-level advisory and coordinating body setting policy direction; PhilSA is the central implementing agency running programs, scholarships, and missions
  3. They are two names for the same office
  4. The Council handles astronomy; PhilSA handles satellites only
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. RA 11363 separates direction from execution: the Council keeps departments aligned at policy level, while PhilSA carries the operational mandate. Knowing which body does what tells a proposer where to seek program partnership (PhilSA) versus where policy coherence is decided (the Council). A inverts the design; C and D erase it.

Question 15 · Applying

A university team proposes a teacher-training program on space science for three school divisions and wants the strongest statutory anchor from this chapter. The best choice is:

  1. RA 10535, because classes start on time
  2. The space education and awareness development area of RA 11363, with PhilSA's education programs as the operational door — statute for the anchor, agency programs for the mechanism
  3. RA 10121, because teachers respond to disasters
  4. No anchor is possible for education projects
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. This is the four-link chain from Chapter 2 grounded in Chapter 3's institutions: a named statutory policy area (education and awareness) plus the implementing agency's live programs. A and C are mechanism-free matches of words — the agenda-washing error transplanted to statutes; D ignores that education is one of the Act's named development areas.

Question 16 · Evaluating

A draft IRR provision (hypothetical) would let the implementing agency add entirely new taxes to fund space programs, though RA 11363 contains no taxing provision. The soundest evaluation is:

  1. Valid — IRRs may improve on statutes
  2. Invalid — taxation requires statutory authority; an IRR operationalizes what the law provides and cannot create new burdens or powers the statute never granted
  3. Valid if the fund runs low
  4. Invalid only if the President objects
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. This is Figure 3.1's conflict rule at its sharpest: delegated rule-making fills in procedure within statutory bounds; imposing new exactions is legislation, which only Congress performs. Need (C) and executive preference (D) cannot substitute for statutory authority — a principle you will meet again when reading funding rules in Weeks 8–10.

Question 17 · Evaluating

A proposal for a national astronomical observatory names PhilSA as sole government stakeholder and never mentions PAGASA. The sharpest reviewer critique, based on this chapter, is:

  1. PhilSA is the wrong agency for anything astronomical
  2. An institutional-overlap blind spot: PAGASA holds the State's legacy astronomical services mandate (RA 10692) while PhilSA holds space science (RA 11363) — a credible proposal maps both mandates and shows coordination with each
  3. Observatories need no government stakeholders
  4. The proposal should have named the NDRRMC instead
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. Astronomy genuinely straddles two statutes: the older service mandate and the newer space-science mandate. Handling the overlap by silence invites the panel's "have you coordinated with…?" question; handling it explicitly — coordination paragraph, letters of support — converts a vulnerability into evidence of institutional literacy. A and D misassign mandates; C ignores how observatories get land, permits, and sustainability.

Question 18 · Applying

Run the statute-to-opportunity mapping (Figure 3.7) for a provincial fireball-alert camera network. Which mapping is correctly matched across all four laws?

  1. RA 10121 → timestamps; RA 10692 → user LGU; RA 10535 → policy anchor; RA 11363 → data source
  2. RA 10121 → user & pillar (LDRRMO, prevention/mitigation); RA 10692 → partner & data (PAGASA); RA 10535 → standard (PhST-disciplined station clocks); RA 11363 → policy anchor & program door (space R&D, PhilSA)
  3. All four laws → funding only
  4. RA 10535 → user; RA 11363 → clock standard; RA 10121 → astronomy mandate; RA 10692 → space council
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. Each statute contributes what it actually contains: DRRM structures and local funds (10121), the meteorological/astronomical service agency (10692), the national timescale (10535), and the space-policy anchor with its implementing agency (11363). A and D shuffle the contributions into nonsense; C flattens four different kinds of infrastructure into money — the exact misreading the mandates/money/standards/constraints framework exists to prevent.

Question 19 · Analyzing

In IRAC terms, the most common cause of lost points in case answers — per this chapter — is:

  1. Writing the issue as a question
  2. A thin Application step: quoting the rule and jumping to a conclusion without matching the scenario's facts to the rule's elements one by one
  3. Using section numbers in the Rule step
  4. Writing a conclusion that answers the question asked
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: B. The Application step is where legal reasoning actually happens — element by element, fact by fact — and it is precisely what the course template's "provide your justification and judgement" demands before the "Therefore…". Options A, C, and D describe correct practice, not errors: framing issues as questions, citing sections, and answering the question asked are exactly what IRAC requires.

Question 20 · Evaluating

Apply full IRAC: A city ordinance declares "City Standard Time," setting clocks 10 minutes ahead of PhST for all city offices, arguing local autonomy. Which answer is the best-formed IRAC evaluation?

  1. "Local autonomy always wins; the ordinance is valid."
  2. "Time zones are a national security matter; the mayor should be prosecuted."
  3. "I: May an LGU establish an official time different from PhST? R: RA 10535 establishes a single Philippine Standard Time, kept by PAGASA, with which government offices must synchronize. A: City offices are government offices; an ordinance creating a divergent local time directly conflicts with the statutory duty to synchronize, and a local ordinance cannot override a national statute. C: Therefore the ordinance is invalid as to official time; the city must synchronize with PhST."
  4. "The ordinance is probably fine because ten minutes is small."
Show correct answer & explanation

Correct answer: C. It is the only option with all four IRAC moves: a precisely framed issue, the governing rule with its elements (single standard, PAGASA keeper, synchronization duty), an application matching facts to each element — including the hierarchy point that ordinances yield to statutes — and a conclusion answering the exact question. A asserts without rule or application; B invents both the rule and the remedy; D substitutes intuition about magnitude for law.
